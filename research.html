Research

My research uses large online experiments to study how people make decisions with information and with AI: when AI helps, when it misleads, and how the design of a tool changes how people rely on it.

Papers

Training Apart, Working Together: How AI Exposure During Learning Shapes Human-AI Complementarity

2026

Single-authored

Does training without AI produce people who are better at working with AI later? In a pre-registered experiment with 901 participants, people learned a classification task with no AI, with an AI assistant, or with the same assistant plus a badge marking its most confident suggestions, and then all worked alongside the same AI. Training with AI did not hurt later performance. The certainty badge did: participants read an unflagged suggestion as a sign the AI was wrong and overrode correct advice, which lowered their earnings.

Beyond the Stars: Welfare Effects of Ratings in Differentiated Markets

2025

with Rustamdjan Hakimov and Luís Santos-Pinto

Star ratings help consumers when everyone agrees which product is better. When products instead suit different tastes, standard ratings do not improve how well people are matched to products. Combining a Bayesian model with a large pre-registered experiment, we show that filtered ratings and algorithmic recommendations recover the lost welfare.

Creative and Strategic Capabilities of Generative AI: Evidence from Large-Scale Experiments

2024

with Rustamdjan Hakimov and Rafael Lalive · Revise & resubmit at Experimental Economics

We compare AI chatbots with a representative sample of US adults. Ideas from ChatGPT were rated more creative than human ideas, and AI assistance raised human creativity, though not to ChatGPT’s level. Raters could not tell AI from human ideas but scored the ones they believed were AI-generated lower. In a strategic game, ChatGPT adapted to its opponent, but humans adapted more successfully on average.
